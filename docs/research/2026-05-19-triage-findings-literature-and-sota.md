## Problem Anchor (Verbatim)

> "An agentic code-review pass now emits a structured set of findings (severity, file:line, why, fix, autofix flag, confidence, machine tally). The open problem is what happens NEXT: triaging those findings — deciding per finding whether to auto-apply, accept-and-fix, reject (false positive / out-of-scope / not-worth-it), defer to a follow-up, or escalate to a human — under conditions of high AI finding volume, modest AI precision, review/alert fatigue, and very limited human reviewing capacity. What do the literature, the craftsmanship/OSS community, and strong-engineering-culture orgs/labs say about how to triage review findings — (a) why triage as a distinct step, (b) what the disposition taxonomy/decision criteria should be, (c) how/process (confidence calibration, false-positive control, dedup/clustering, batching, auto-fix routing, human-in-the-loop placement, escalation), (d) when/how often / where in the pipeline it sits, (e) degrees of autonomy and the safe envelope for autonomous disposition vs mandatory human gate — first in the pre-agentic era, then the agentic-era SOTA as of May 2026? Synthesise into actionable, evidence-graded guidance for designing a triage step suitable for a senior SOLO developer maximising AI-agent leverage without self-harm. Include a falsifiable recommendation on (i) whether triage should be a standalone factored-out skill vs an inline step, and (ii) what the autonomous-vs-human-gate boundary should be."

### Resolution Criteria (Pre-Committed, Before Results Seen)

A strong answer must: (1) separate *stated rationale* from *empirically measured outcome* for "why a distinct triage step"; (2) give a provenance-tagged disposition taxonomy and decision criteria, not a flat list; (3) distinguish triage placement/cadence options with fit conditions; (4) treat vendor claims as marketing until independently corroborated; (5) deliver a falsifiable recommendation on standalone-skill-vs-inline AND on the autonomous/human-gate boundary, with trade-offs; (6) grade every load-bearing claim by evidence strength and flag single-source/unverifiable ones. Evidence that would change the conclusion: a credible independent result showing autonomous LLM disposition of its own/another model's findings is reliable enough to drop the human gate; a replication showing finding-clustering/dedup materially changes triage *accuracy*; strong evidence that a separate triage agent/context beats inline same-context triage (or the reverse).

## Thesis

**Triage is not a deliberation; it is a filter that protects a finite human attention budget — and every mature domain that has faced machine-generated finding volume independently evolved away from a per-item deliberative middle toward a fast automatic zone plus a small bounded human zone.** The pre-agentic adjacent literatures (bug triage, SAST false-positive management, SOC alert fatigue, code-review-comment usefulness) and the agentic SOTA converge on this from opposite directions, exactly mirroring the convergence thesis of the prior code-review research. Three corollaries force most of this document:

1. **The value of a triage step is measured in human attention *not* spent**, so a triage step that itself demands per-finding human deliberation has failed on its own terms.
2. **Automatic disposition is safe for classification against fixed criteria and for deterministic/mechanical action; it is structurally unsafe for open correctness re-judgement and for irreversible/semantic change** — and the cost of wrongly auto-rejecting a real defect dominates every other triage error.
3. **A multi-bucket disposition taxonomy is itself the deliberative middle the thesis warns against; its only defensible purpose is transitional** — a scaffold that accumulates the per-codebase calibration memory which lets the system *collapse* toward the two-zone terminus (deterministic auto-dispose / surface-to-human-with-a-recommended-disposition). The engineering brief is therefore "build the minimum system that generates the calibration data to eventually need less system," not "build a rich triage engine." Every domain surveyed has already walked this collapse (medical triage: 576 reasons → ESI's 5 → go-now/wait); the design should anticipate it from day one rather than rediscover it under fatigue.

## Part 1 — the Pre-Agentic Era (Adjacent Literatures, Mined for Transferable Principles)

There is no pre-agentic literature on "triaging AI code-review findings" specifically. There are four mature adjacent bodies of evidence. They were mined for *transferable principles*, with provenance and the limits of transfer stated.

### 1.1 Why Triage as a Distinct Step (Stated Rationale Vs Measured Outcome)

- **Stated rationale (everywhere):** volume exceeds human capacity; disposition types are heterogeneous and require different follow-on actions; routing the right item to the right place reduces total cost.
- **Measured outcome (the durable finding):** the *primary measurable value of triage is human attention not consumed* and *false-negatives avoided via fatigue reduction* — not classification accuracy itself. SOC research treats alert fatigue as a measured security vulnerability (≈3,000 alerts/day, 42–63% uninvestigated, 46% FP, 61% of teams have ignored alerts later confirmed genuine; Tariq et al. ACM CSUR 2025 ▰▰▰ for the mechanism, vendor surveys ▰▰ for the magnitudes). Static-analysis research independently established the same: developers abandon tools wholesale past an FP threshold (Johnson et al. ICSE 2013, 698+ citations ▰▰▰; Bessey et al. CACM 2010 ▰▰▰).
- **The economic asymmetry:** in bug triage, *mis-routing* — not fix difficulty — is the dominant delay (bug "tossing" costs 100–250 days, Jeong et al. FSE 2009 ▰▰▰; Coverity alerts take ~3 months to fix despite a median fix of ~3 lines, Imtiaz et al. ISSRE 2019 ▰▰ — web-verified 2026-05-19). The bottleneck is the disposition decision, not the fix.

Skill implication: frame triage's success metric as **attention saved and real defects not silently dropped**, not as disposition-classification accuracy.

### 1.2 the Disposition Taxonomy and Decision Criteria — with Provenance

Convergent practitioner taxonomies (no academic canonical one exists):

| SAST / SARIF                  | accepted · underReview · rejected; suppression kind ∈ {inSource, external}                            | OASIS SARIF v2.1.0 ▰▰▰ (standards body)                      |

Per-finding decision criteria, distilled from the code-review-comment-usefulness literature (the most direct analogue), each with provenance and strength:

1. **Commit-introduced (scope gate)** — was this introduced/worsened by the change under review? Bosu MSR 2015 (change-proximity) ▰▰▰; Codex prompt criterion 4 ▰.
2. **Concrete impact (severity gate)** — affects correctness/security/perf/maintainability, not taste. Mäntylä & Lassenius TSE 2009 (75% of findings are evolvability) ▰▰▰; resolution rates by type: Bugs 41.9% / Readability 43.3% / Design 28.6% (Atlassian RovoDev 2025 ▰▰ — *its own curated product, team-context, human resolving AI comments; not a triage agent's disposition and not solo-dev*). Note: this 28–43% band and emergent-pattern-1's "AI comments 0.9–19.2%" are *different studies measuring different things* — Atlassian RovoDev's own curated output vs Yüksel et al. 2026's cross-13-agent comparative; both are team-context, transfer to solo-dev is extrapolation, and the gap between them is itself evidence that AI-finding actionability is highly system- and curation-dependent.
3. **Discrete & actionable** — pins to a location and implies a concrete action. Rahman MSR 2017; LLM noise-detection precision 66–85% ▰▰.
4. **Author would act (intent filter)** — consistent with the change's intent and the codebase's existing rigour. Turzo & Bosu 2023 ▰▰▰ for the extended "useful" definition (incl. explicit deferral as a positive outcome); the counterfactual itself **not validated** ▰.
5. **Not assumption-dependent (context validity)** — establishable from the diff without unstated assumptions; AI agents generate FPs precisely by assuming external contracts (Zheng et al. 2026 ▰▰).
6. **Type as routing signal** — functional-defect > readability/bug > maintainability > design/discussion > question/praise; resolution rate tracks this order (Zahedi ESEM 2023; Atlassian 2025 ▰▰).
7. **Calibrated to *this* codebase** — generic-threshold models are systematically mis-wired; within-project ≫ cross-project (Novais et al. EMSE 2025 ▰▰▰).
8. **Volume/fatigue context** — more findings per change ⇒ lower proportion acted on; the queue length itself is a triage input (Bosu 2015 ▰▰▰).

### 1.3 How — Process Mechanics (The Transferable Operating Principles)

- **Precision is a trust threshold, not a preference (the do-not-surface doctrine).** Tricorder's "effective false positive" = any finding the developer does not act on, *regardless of technical correctness*. Surfacing a weak finding costs trust proportional to the finding's prominence, not its actual FP probability. ▰▰▰. → triage's first job is suppression, and suppression must be *visible/audited*, not silent. Two distinct Schäfer et al. FSE 2025 findings (web-verified 2026-05-19, ▰▰▰): (a) **50.8% of suppression annotations are ineffective** — they no longer affect any active warning (stale/mis-placed, not necessarily deceptive); (b) **separately, suppressions — including ineffective ones — can unintentionally hide *future* warnings**. The lesson is the same regardless of mechanism: an unaudited suppression is a silent future blind spot.
- **Cluster/dedup before disposition, not after** (Fry & Weimer WCRE 2013 ▰▰; SOC incident-correlation ▰▰) — but as a cheap presentation device at solo scale (see Finding 11).
- **Calibrate by feedback, not by static rules.** Z-ranking / Feedback-Rank: developer dispositions on inspected findings are the highest-signal input for ranking the rest (2–8× over random; Kremenek & Engler SAS 2003 ▰▰▰). The production analogue is a disposition memory.
- **Baseline/diff-only.** Introducing a finding stream over a legacy codebase without a baseline causes immediate trust collapse; enforce on *new* findings only (PVS-Studio/Android-Lint convergent practice ▰▰). TAD already does this structurally (review is diff-scoped).
- **Tiered triage with escalation by uncertainty, not by category.** SOC L1/L2/L3: L1 may auto-close only *documented* benign patterns; anything novel/ambiguous escalates regardless of apparent severity. High-severity *false positives* are the most destructive — they train distrust of the high-severity channel — so the human gate belongs at the *uncertainty* boundary, not merely the severity boundary (Tricorder, SOC, APR all converge ▰▰).
- **Explainability is a prerequisite for trust, not a nicety.** Practitioners override ML dispositions lacking a natural-language rationale (Lind et al. EMSE 2024 ▰▰). Every disposition must carry a one-line reason.

### 1.4 When / Where in the Pipeline

- **At the change/review moment, not a deferred dashboard** (Tricorder ▰▰▰; Vassallo et al. EMSE 2020 ▰▰).
- **Cadence has no published norm for code-review findings.** SOC correlates in real time; bug triage is continuous; spam/content-moderation use a deferred quarantine batch. The transferable rule is the *bounded queue with a TTL*, not a specific interval (see emergent pattern E3).

### 1.5 the Cost Models

- Triage cost is *router-dependent*, not just item-dependent (CosTriage AAAI 2011: cost-aware routing −30% total cost ▰▰). The agentic analogue: route trivial findings to a cheap deterministic lane; reserve expensive judgement (model or human) for high-blast-radius items.
- Ground-truth labels are structurally noisy: ~51% of duplicate bug reports carry inconsistent human severity labels (Tian et al. EMSE 2015 ▰▰▰). Any severity/confidence score — human or LLM — inherits this; treat scores as ordinal filters, not probabilities.

**Convergent disposition architecture (≥3 independent-lineage systems, ▰▰▰):** (1) a verify/judge/coordinator pass is the *primary* FP control — universal; (2) 3–5 severity tiers map to disposition; (3) an explicit "do-NOT-flag" negative-constraint list; (4) suppression of nit/P2-P3 class in high-volume contexts; (5) bias toward approval / "no findings is valid"; (6) human-authored repo-context files as the tuning surface; (7) reviewer ≠ fixer in the disposition step itself.

### 2.2 the Calibration Reality

- **LLM verbalised confidence is not a calibrated probability.** Global Platt-scaling "remains unreliable for automated code revision" (arXiv:2604.06723, single preprint ▰▰). The widely-copied 80 threshold is a design heuristic from one team, inherited by TAD; no public study measures the true-positive fraction among score-≥80 findings in production. Architectural verification (coordinator reads the source; judge agent traces) may be more reliable than a self-assessed number precisely because it substitutes tool-grounded evidence for introspection.
- **The only thing that beats the cross-project wall is per-project memory.** Within-project models robustly beat cross-project across every adjacent literature (▰▰▰). Production systems operationalise this as accumulated disposition memory (Semgrep memories auto-triage 60% at 96% researcher-agreement — vendor ▰▰; BugBot 44k learned rules; CodeRabbit learnings). This is the agentic form of Feedback-Rank.
- **Resolution rate ≠ precision.** Vendors report "% of findings fixed before merge" (BugBot 76%, Greptile 43%). A developer fixing a false positive to clear the queue inflates this without improving triage quality. No independent benchmark measures *post-filter* (disposition) precision separately from raw detection — this does not exist as of May 2026 (gap).

### 2.3 the Autonomy Boundary — What Is Empirically Safe to Automate

- **Autonomy-level taxonomies converge** (Swarmia, ASDLC, Knight-Columbia, Data-Agents L0–L5; all practitioner/pre-print ▰▰): L1–L2 advisory/suggest (human per item); L3 conditional autonomy (human reviews *output*, not each suggestion); L4 autonomous detect-and-repair (human on escalation only); L5 observer. Current production ceiling L3–L4; the L2→L3 jump (suggest → auto-apply) is the load-bearing gate.
- **Safe to automate (deterministic, not confidence-based):** drops mandated by the do-not-flag list; verify-before-post failures (no file:line / no traced causal chain); dedup collapse; mechanically-certain reversible text transforms with CI green. This is the empirically-supported abstention form because it does not depend on model self-calibration.
- **Not safe to automate without a pre-authorised per-class policy + CI green:** any semantic/behavioural fix. The only published semantic auto-fix regression rate is Snyk Agent Fix ~5.3% — explicitly "too high for autonomous merge, appropriate for human-in-the-loop PR" (single field test ▰). GitHub, with the most autofix data, keeps a human gate (revealed preference ▰▰). APR evidence: tool/AI *provenance labels inflate human acceptance regardless of correctness* (Papotti et al. EMSE 2024 ▰▰) — so showing the human a label without the evidence systematically over-applies.
- **Never auto-*reject*:** any Critical/Major, regardless of model confidence — the cost-asymmetry veto (Finding 5). Auto-rejection of a real high-severity finding is the single highest-cost triage error and it is silent.
- **Self-disposition caveat (the design's load-bearing assumption):** a model triaging its own findings performs a form of self-correction and inherits self-preference + framing bias; the disposition-specific magnitude is unmeasured but the prior is strong. The proposed mitigation — constrain the disposition decision to **classification against fixed criteria** (scope / severity / mechanical-fix-available / do-not-flag-match), never open "is this finding correct?" re-judgement — rests on a **structural analogy (▰)**, not a measured result: arXiv:2511.09381 tests multiple-choice answer-selection on NLU/reasoning tasks (not triage routing) and finds MC merely "can leverage clearer solution boundaries but may be limited by the provided options." Inline triage is therefore *defensible only if that analogy holds for routing decisions*; the boundary it implies could be too conservative (over-escalating) or too permissive (self-favouring under framing). This is flagged here, not only in the pre-mortem, because every downstream tier inherits it.

### 2.4 Reviewing AI-Generated Findings About AI-Generated Code — the Compounding Case

The diff is usually agent-authored *and* the findings are agent-generated *and* the triager is an agent. Each layer has measured low precision (generation F1 19–51% independent; adoption 16.6%; >50% of unadopted AI suggestions wrong). Disposition precision cannot in expectation exceed generation precision. Consequences already partly in the prior research, sharpened here: keep the human on the consequential minority; make the autonomous lanes deterministic; treat any AI-proposed fix as a candidate the human ratifies at the existing gate, never a silent merge.

## The Two Decisions (Answered Explicitly)

### Decision (I) — Standalone Factored-Out Skill, or Inline Step?

**Recommendation (picks a side): build the thin standalone `triage` *artifact* skill. One default, stated unhedged. It is justified by single-responsibility alone (not reuse); scoped to code-review-style findings only; kept minimal — see Decision (ii), it is closer to a deterministic routing function than a reasoning engine. The conditional variant, *not* a co-equal: if within the first handful of real `auto-task` runs the skill's routing turns out fully determined by fields `code-review` already emits (severity/type/autofix-flag) with an empty residual, collapse it to inline + a shared `docs/triage-rubric.md` — same content, no invocable surface. Default = skill; fallback = rubric-doc only on that specific trigger.**

Grounding in the repo's own factoring doctrine (`docs/decisions.md`, 2026-05-11): artifact skills own a contract+procedure; orchestration skills coordinate; don't mix them or you get a god-object. The repo's stated trigger is "≥2 reuse sites + single-responsibility boundary + stable contract" — but be honest about which leg actually bears weight here. The reuse leg is **weak**: auto-task Step 7.2 is *internal* reuse of Step 6.1's prose within one skill (not an independent call site), and review-loop's pushback table is *already its own construct* with a different contract (the pre-mortem concedes both). Counting these as "≥2 sites" is close to circular. The leg that bears weight is **single-responsibility**: triage is a classify-and-route step in the same family as `code-review` (classify-and-emit), and `code-review` already explicitly disclaims the routing role ("the skill classifies and emits, it does not decide workflow — the caller routes"). Triage *is* that caller; leaving it as unnamed prose inside auto-task is the god-object the doctrine warns against. Assessment:

Decisive supporting reasons: a named skill (a) makes the do-not-flag/verify/autonomy contract a single auditable surface instead of prose duplicated across auto-task and review-loop; (b) gives the calibration memory one home.

**The conditional variant (one named trigger, not a co-equal default):** the *only* condition that flips the choice to "inline + `docs/triage-rubric.md`" is empirical and specific — if, across the first handful of real runs, every finding's disposition is fully determined by fields `code-review` already emits (severity / type / `Autofix:` flag) with a zero residual needing judgement, then the "skill" is a pure routing function and an invocable LLM surface earns nothing; collapse it. Same rubric content either way, so the decision is low-regret and reversible — but the default until that trigger fires is the skill.

**Falsifiable / where the skill is wrong:** falsified if the above trigger fires (residual judgement ≈ 0 across early runs ⇒ it should have been a rubric-doc + caller conditional), *or* if a second genuine code-finding call site never materialises within the first ~10 auto-task runs (then single-responsibility alone did not earn a separate surface and inline+rubric was correct). Note this is now an *independent* falsifier — the earlier "review-task adapter" condition was vacuous because the hard scoping rule already excludes review-task. Hard scoping rule: **the contract covers code-review-style findings only**; review-task keeps its own lighter inline disposition (absorbing both is the god-object). Constraints any version must honour: emits a table + autofix batch, never spawns the fixer (reviewer≠fixer; subagent-has-no-Agent-tool); `Autofix:` routing token must survive `/synthesise` verbatim (make this an explicit synthesise contract); out-of-scope follow-ups → task-file notes, never auto-filed GitHub issues; the task file is a mandatory input.

### Decision (Ii) — the Autonomous-Vs-Human-Gate Boundary

**Recommendation (the lead, unhedged — this is what to build): a deterministic routing function, not an LLM deliberation pass.** The doc's own premises force this: confidence scores are uncalibrated (§2.2), self-assessment is biased (Finding 6), only fixed-criteria classification is defensibly autonomous (Finding 6), and the cost-asymmetry veto is absolute for Critical/Major (Finding 5). Together these mean the disposition of the *bulk* of findings is fully determined by three fields `code-review` **already emits** — severity, type, and the `Autofix:` flag — so for the bulk **triage needs no LLM reasoning at all**: it is a lookup/filter in the caller. An LLM (or human) judgement is required only for the *residual* — the "real but not worth it / out of scope" calls — and that residual is exactly the consequential minority that must go to the human anyway. So the honest answer to "does triage need an LLM?" is: **no for the bulk (pure routing), yes only for a residual that should be surfaced, not auto-decided.**

**Therefore the MVP *is* the recommendation, stated first and plainly (a solo dev should build exactly this and nothing more):**

1. **T0 deterministic drops** — do-not-flag matches, verify-before-post failures, dedup collapse. No model judgement.
2. **T0 mechanically-certain autofix** — the existing `Autofix:`+Minor/Nit bypass. No model judgement.
3. **Everything else → the existing ship-gate**, each item carrying a one-line recommended disposition + the traced evidence. The human decides the residual.

That is three rules. It is the whole day-one design. **T1 (autonomous accept→fix) is NOT day-one** — it is a future, transitional optimisation, built per-class only after accumulated disposition memory proves a class safe, and it carries the highest abandonment risk in the system (T1-precision contagion — see pre-mortem). The full tier table below is a *design-space map for later*, not the build order. Building past the three rules before calibration data exists is the premature complexity the thesis exists to warn against.

"Escalate" collapses to "surface at the existing ship-gate," not a mid-pipeline stop (the solo dev is the only escalation target; auto-task's mandate is high autonomy) — valid *only* if the ship-gate queue is bounded (see hard rules); an unbounded gate is the same graveyard E3 identifies everywhere else.

*Design-space map for later (NOT the build order — see the three-rule MVP above):*

| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **T0 Auto-suppress/route (deterministic)** | do-not-flag drops; verify-before-post failures; dedup/cluster collapse; below-threshold bucket                                    | Fully autonomous — no model-confidence dependence                                                         | Findings 3, 6; SAST do-not-surface ▰▰▰      |

Hard rules: **never auto-reject a Critical/Major** (silent, highest-cost, irreversible-in-effect). **Never auto-apply a semantic fix without CI green + pre-authorised class.** Show the human the *evidence*, not just a confidence label (APR provenance-inflation ▰▰). The defer bucket must have a concrete destination + cap, or it must not exist (emergent pattern E3). **The T2 ship-gate queue is itself bounded:** apply E3 to the design's own escalation target, or "escalate → ship-gate" just relocates the graveyard. Cap T2 at a small N — **an arbitrary initial bound, not an evidence-derived number** (kaizen's ≤5 is an internal design echo, not independent confirmation; the nearest *adjacent-literature* anchors are SRE's "2–3 actionable incidents per shift" and Tricorder's EFP-budget logic, both of which point to "single digits," not a precise value). Treat N≈5 as a provisional prior to **validate empirically per-PR**: raise it only if genuine T2 items routinely hit the cap, lower it if dwell time falls as approval rate rises. Past the cap, "explicit per-item acknowledgement" must mean something *operationally different* from normal batch ship-gate review — at minimum a forced per-item pause that cannot be cleared with a single approve-all action; if it collapses to the same gesture as normal review, the cap is theatre. Overflow is a signal the *upstream review* is mis-tuned, not that the human should read faster.

**The enforcement-vacuum gap (stated, not solved).** In a team, peer review and the reviewer-≠-author separation enforce non-rubber-stamping. A solo dev at a high-autonomy ship-gate has *no such enforcement* — the structural risk is silent drift into batch-approval, which would hollow out the entire veto. This research does not resolve it; it flags it as the central open design problem for the solo-dev case (Open Question 3) and offers only proxies: track ship-gate per-item dwell time and approval rate; if approval rate → ~100% with falling dwell time, autonomy is mis-calibrated and T1 should contract, not expand. This is a *detection* heuristic, not a guarantee — no literature establishes a solo-dev rubber-stamp threshold. **Stated honestly: the three-rule MVP, by design, surfaces *more* at the gate (no T1 to absorb low-risk items), so it is the configuration most exposed to this gap.** That is an accepted degraded-mode tradeoff, not a solved problem: the MVP relies solely on the ≤5 T2 cap + the dwell/approval-rate detection heuristic, and accepts residual rubber-stamp risk in exchange for not building the (higher-contagion-risk) T1 prematurely. The honest conclusion is that the enforcement vacuum makes the *full* T1/T2 design premature to ship for a solo dev, not that the gap is closed — it is the strongest single reason the recommendation is "MVP only, until evidence."

**Falsifiable / what would move the boundary:** unlock autonomous disposition of Critical/Major only if a controlled study shows same-/cross-model self-disposition reaches >90% precision *and* recall, or a deployed (non-vendor) system shows a semantic auto-apply regression rate below the project's tolerated defect-injection rate. That tolerated rate is *not* asserted here as a universal number — it must be set per project from acceptable-defect-rate reasoning (for TAD's silent-corruption domain it is near-zero, which is itself the argument against semantic auto-apply). The only published reference point is Snyk Agent Fix's ~5.3% semantic-fix regression — **a single-org field test, ▰, no error bars, not a universal threshold** — judged in that test "too high for autonomous merge." Combined with independent-benchmark generation F1 of 19–51% (a triager's disposition precision cannot in expectation exceed the generation precision it operates on) and the non-existence of any triage-precision benchmark, the boundary is nowhere near movable on current evidence.

### Emergent Patterns (No Single Agent Owned These)

1. **The "15% is the spec" pattern recurs one level up.** The prior research found "only ~15% of review comments are defects" became the design brief for *what to review*. Here, "even human review comments trigger a change only ~30–45% of the time; AI comments 0.9–19%" becomes the brief for *triage*: its job is to surface the actionable minority and make the majority disappear **without spending human attention**. Triage is the operationalisation of the actionability base rate.
2. **Every mature domain independently evolved a 2-zone structure — fast lossy auto-zone + small bounded human zone — and explicitly *away from* a deliberative middle.** Medical (immediate vs not), Toyota jidoka (normal flow vs stop-the-line), TCAS (TA advisory vs RA mandatory), SRE (log vs page), SOC (auto-action vs escalate), spam (auto vs quarantine). This is the most independent finding in the corpus and it is the spine of the proposal: **a per-finding multi-bucket human deliberation table is the thing every domain that faced this problem stopped doing.** Pushed to its conclusion: the proposed 5-bucket disposition taxonomy is *itself* an instance of the evolutionary stage every domain passed through (medical: 576 reasons → ESI 5 → go-now/wait). It is therefore not the answer — it is the *scaffold that produces the calibration data needed to collapse to the answer*. A triage skill that treats 5 buckets as the destination has frozen itself at the stage the field outgrew; one that treats them as transitional and instruments each class for promotion (→ deterministic T0) or demotion (→ human T2) is building toward the terminus. This reframes the engineering brief from "build a good triage system" to "build the smallest system that earns its own obsolescence."
3. **The bounded queue / TTL is load-bearing and universally under-specified.** Content moderation, spam, and SOC all show an unbounded "defer" bin becomes a graveyard and collapses into rubber-stamping. TAD's current "out of scope → capture as follow-up" is exactly this under-specified bin (internal-fit §5.5). Any defer disposition needs a hard destination + a cap, or it should be deleted.
4. **Calibration is impossible without memory; memory is the only thing that beats the cross-project wall.** Within-project ≫ cross-project everywhere. TAD has a *latent* version — "recurring autofix class → codify as a linter/CLAUDE.md rule." That sentence is the calibration loop; it should be generalised from autofix-only to recurring-FP-class and recurring-reject-class, written to project rules, not a private store.
5. **Deskilling is the unmeasured long-horizon, solo-dev-specific risk.** Every other failure mode is about this PR; deskilling is the erosion of the human's capacity to be the backstop over months (automation-bias / illusion-of-competence literature; no code-review longitudinal study exists). The mitigation is dosage — keep the human on the consequential minority so judgement doesn't atrophy — which is the *same* design the cost-asymmetry argument independently produces. Convergence is convenient; flag it as a hypothesis, not a proven control.
6. **The sharpest result: for the bulk of findings, triage is a pure routing function, not an LLM step.** Four of this document's own premises — confidence is uncalibrated; self-assessment is biased; only fixed-criteria classification is defensibly autonomous; the cost-asymmetry veto is absolute — *jointly entail* that the disposition of most findings is fully determined by attributes `code-review` **already emits** (severity, type, `Autofix:` flag). Nothing about that requires a fresh LLM reasoning pass; it is a deterministic lookup in the caller. The *only* part that needs judgement is the "real but out-of-scope / not-worth-it" residual — and that residual is precisely the consequential minority that must be surfaced to the human anyway, *not* auto-decided. So the strongest design statement the corpus supports is: **triage does not need an LLM for what it can safely automate, and must not use one for what it cannot.** The LLM-shaped "triage agent" is the deliberative middle in disguise; the correct artefact is a routing table plus a one-line human-facing recommendation on the residual. This is sharper than the 2-zone framing and dissolves much of the apparent design surface. One boundary qualifier (so the claim is not over-read): "pure routing" describes the *steady-state, calibrated* system. *Applying* the do-not-flag list to a novel or partially-matching finding, and the cold-start case (no accumulated memory yet), are not deterministic — but the design needs no new machinery for them: the uncertainty-escalation rule (§1.3) already routes any ambiguous match to the human, and the cost-asymmetry veto forbids auto-rejecting Critical/Major regardless. Edge cases fall through to the human by the rules already stated; they are not a separate mechanism, just an explicitly-acknowledged fallthrough.

## Pre-Mortem — Assume the Main Conclusions Are Wrong

- **If "factor out a thin standalone skill" is wrong:** the reuse is illusory — Step 7.2's input is structurally different, review-loop's pushback table is already its own construct, kaizen deliberately doesn't fix. Then the right move is *not* a skill: tighten Step 6.1 inline and extract only the shared *rubric* (do-not-flag + disposition buckets + autonomy tiers) as a referenced doc both auto-task and review-loop point at. The decision hinges on whether ≥2 genuine same-contract call sites survive scoping to code-findings-only. Depend on the single-responsibility argument, not the reuse count.
- **If "mostly-automatic thin router" is wrong:** it fails if AI precision is so low that the auto-accept→fix lane churns the codebase with wrong fixes (16.6% adoption; >50% unadopted wrong; Snyk 5.3% ▰). The design degrades safely *because* the auto lanes are deterministic/mechanical + CI-green and semantic accepts are ratified at the existing ship-gate, never silent-merged. If even that churns, shrink T1 to empty and keep only T0 + human veto.
- **The most dangerous attack — T1 precision contagion (the failure mode the whole design exists to prevent):** T1's "low-blast-radius / unambiguous / in-scope" gate is *itself a correctness-adjacent judgement*, not a fixed-criteria classification, so it inherits the self-disposition risk (Finding 6, ▰). If T1's classification precision sits below the (unmeasured, solo-dev) actionability cliff, T1 doesn't just churn — it *poisons trust in T0 by contagion*: once the developer catches the agent auto-applying a wrong "safe" fix, they stop trusting the deterministic T0 lane too, and revert to reading everything, which is exactly the wholesale-disengagement (Finding 1) the system was built to prevent. This is why T1 is transitional and gated behind earned per-class memory, not a day-one feature, and why the MVP deliberately omits it. The design's safety rests on T1 being *small, slow to grow, and instrumented* — if it is built eagerly, it is the single most likely cause of total system abandonment.
- **If "human gate at the existing ship-gate is enough" is wrong:** two ways. (a) A real Critical is auto-rejected as FP before the human sees it — mitigated by the hard veto: Critical/Major are *never* auto-rejected, only "route to fixer" or "surface to human." (b) The gate exists but the solo dev rubber-stamps it under load (the enforcement-vacuum gap) — *not* fully mitigated; the design offers only the bounded-T2 cap + dwell-time/approval-rate detection heuristic and an honest admission that no literature establishes a solo-dev rubber-stamp threshold. If (b) dominates, the veto is theatre and the only real protection is keeping T2 small enough that attention doesn't collapse — which is why the T2 bound is a hard rule, not a guideline.
- **If the 2-zone thesis is wrong:** it would require a controlled study showing a deliberative multi-bucket triage outperforms a binary severity gate for AI findings. No such study exists — the null (binary gate suffices) is unfalsified in either direction (contrarian §4). The proposal hedges by keeping the human-zone *bounded and small*, which is robust whichever way that null resolves.
- **Fragile assumptions, do not hard-depend on any single one:** solo-dev tolerance for the actionability cliff is *entirely unmeasured* (all studies are team-context); "self-correction degradation extends to disposition" is *analogy, not measurement*; the CCR magnitude is small-n single-study; the deskilling risk has no code-review longitudinal evidence; the strongest autonomy numbers (Snyk 5.3%, BugBot resolution-rate, Semgrep 60%/96%) are single-source or vendor.

## Implications for a Triage Skill (The Brief)

**The three-rule nucleus — build exactly this, nothing more, on day one:** (1) deterministic T0 drops (do-not-flag match, verify-fail, dedup collapse); (2) the existing mechanically-certain `Autofix:` bypass; (3) everything else → the existing ship-gate carrying a one-line recommended disposition + traced evidence. This is a routing function, not an LLM pass (emergent pattern 6). Implications 3–12 below are the *design-space map and the deferred optimisations* — add any one only when a specific calibrated need is observed, never speculatively. A solo dev who implements 1–3 and stops has the correct system; one who implements all 12 up front has re-imported the fatigue the thesis exists to remove (implication 12 applies to this list itself).

**Reading the strength grades for action:** a ▰ finding (e.g. the classification-is-safer mitigation, Snyk 5.3%) means *acceptable to rely on at MVP scale, with an explicit revisit trigger* — not "ignore" and not "trust." A ▰▰▰ finding (cost-asymmetry veto, fatigue-coupling) is a hard constraint to design against. The grades are actionable instructions, not just epistemic hedges.

Ordered by leverage.

1. **Triage is a router, not a deliberation.** Its success metric is human attention *not* spent + zero real defects silently dropped. If a triage decision needs as much judgement as the fix, it is review, not triage — escalate it, don't deliberate it inline.
2. **Two zones, no deliberative middle — and the 5-bucket taxonomy is transitional scaffolding toward them, not the terminus.** Steady state: a *deterministic* auto-zone (do-not-flag drops, verify-fail drops, dedup collapse, mechanically-certain autofix) and a small **bounded** human zone (everything Critical/Major + security/concurrency/API/irreversible + cross-file-semantic + low-consistency). The five disposition buckets exist only to *generate the per-class memory* that lets each class migrate to one of the two zones; design them to be collapsed, not curated. T1 (autonomous accept→fix) is a transitional lane, never steady-state, and is built only after memory justifies a specific class.
   - **MVP first:** T0-suppress + T0-mechanical-autofix + "everything else → ship-gate with a recommended disposition." Ship that; do not build T1 or a memory subsystem until the data exists to warrant it.
3. **The do-not-flag list is the spine here too** — it governs disposition more than any positive rubric (the prior research's Finding #8, independently reconfirmed by the SAST raw-signal evidence). Triage's first pass is suppression.
4. **The cost-asymmetry veto is absolute:** never auto-reject Critical/Major; bias to over-flag severity; auto-reject only on deterministic grounds (do-not-flag match, verify-fail). A silently dropped real defect is the worst outcome in the system.
5. **Constrain inline triage to classification against fixed criteria** (scope / severity / mechanical-fix-available / do-not-flag-match / multi-pass-consistency). Never inline-ask "is this finding correct?" — that is the self-correction-degraded judgement; route it to the human or to fresh-context verification.
6. **Disposition needs a one-line rationale + the traced evidence**, not just a confidence label (provenance-inflation evidence). Confidence is an ordinal filter, never a probability.
7. **The defer bucket must have a destination and a cap** or be deleted. For TAD: out-of-scope → task-file `## Implementation notes` (or an explicit user offer), never an auto-filed GitHub issue; bounded count; surfaced at the ship-gate.
8. **Calibrate via memory, generalised from the existing latent loop:** recurring autofix *and* recurring FP/reject classes → propose codification (linter rule / architecture test / CLAUDE.md rule). This is the only lever that beats the cross-project wall.
9. **Keep the human in the loop at the *existing* gates** (auto-task Step 9 ship-gate, Step 7.3), not a new mid-pipeline stop — this respects the high-autonomy mandate while preserving the veto. "Escalate" for a solo dev = "surface here with a recommended disposition," not "block and wait."
10. **Don't build dedup/clustering as a subsystem at solo scale** — a cheap prose-level "these N are one root cause, fix once" grouping is sufficient; revisit only if per-run finding volume structurally exceeds eyeball range.
11. **Phase autonomy, don't front-load it.** Advisory recommended-disposition first; widen the autonomous lanes only for finding classes whose disposition has proven stable in the codebase's accumulated memory.
12. **Keep the skill tight.** The nervousness heuristic from the prior research applies doubly: a triage skill that grows a rich taxonomy is re-importing the fatigue it exists to remove.

### TAD-Specific Appendix (Portable Core Above)

Current state: `auto-task` Step 6.1 already implements a defensible v0 — the `Autofix:`+Minor/Nit deterministic bypass (T0) and a 5-bucket Reject/Accept table against the task-intent anchor, autonomous within auto-task's mandate, human veto at Steps 7.3/9. The gaps this research identifies, in priority order: (a) the 5-bucket table is presented as a per-finding deliberation — reframe as a router with the buckets as *routing outcomes*, most auto-decided, only T2 surfaced; (b) the defer/out-of-scope bucket is unbounded and under-specified — give it a hard destination + cap; (c) the calibration loop is autofix-only — generalise to recurring FP/reject classes; (d) the `Autofix:`-survives-`/synthesise` contract is implicit — make it explicit; (e) "never auto-reject Critical/Major" is implied by defence-in-depth but should be stated as the cost-asymmetry veto; (f) factor the rubric into a thin `triage` skill so auto-task Step 6.1 and review-loop Step 3 share one auditable contract, scoped to code-review findings only (review-task keeps its lighter inline disposition). TAD constraints any change must honour: reviewer≠fixer; subagent-has-no-Agent-tool (triage emits a table, the fixer is a separately-spawned subagent); never auto-file GitHub issues; the task file is the mandatory intent anchor; silent corruption findings are Critical/Major and therefore always T2.

## Open Questions and What Was Not Found

1. **No triage-specific precision/recall benchmark exists** (post-filter disposition quality, separate from raw detection) as of May 2026. Every benchmark (SWR-Bench, Martian, SWE-PRBench) measures detection.
2. **"Self-correction degradation extends to disposition" is unmeasured.** The analogy from Huang et al. is structurally sound but no study uses triage-decision accuracy as the dependent variable. The single most important open question for this design.
3. **Solo-dev tolerance for the actionability cliff is entirely unmeasured.** Every adjacent study is team-context with peer-review social pressure; what replaces that enforcement mechanism for a solo dev is extrapolation.
4. **No RCT of "triage step vs no triage step" for AI code-review findings.** The null (binary severity gate suffices) is unfalsified in either direction.
5. **Deskilling from automated triage has no code-review longitudinal evidence.** Clinical-AI deskilling exists; the transfer is assumed.
6. **No published norm for triage cadence / batch interval** for code-review findings (SOC is real-time, spam is quarantine-batch — no code-review analogue measured).
7. **Cold-start calibration without history is unaddressed** — every memory/feedback mechanism (Z-ranking, Semgrep memories) assumes accumulated dispositions; the solo-dev starts at zero.
8. **The "author would fix it if they knew" counterfactual is never tested independently** — only the change-triggering proxy is.
9. Vendor numbers (Semgrep 60%/96%, BugBot resolution-rate, CodeRabbit/Greptile, Cloudflare) are single-source/self-reported; some 2026 arXiv IDs are pre-print and unreplicated. CCR and arXiv:2603.15911 were web-verified in the prior research round.
